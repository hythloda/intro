Featured App Qualification and Guidance

APPROVED: October 22, 2026

Effective Dates

Marker Guidance Effective as of: 2 weeks after approval by Tokenomics.

$0.79 App Rewards Cap: SV voting to be scheduled to align with Marker Guidance.

Traffic Based Version: As per implementation timelines in amended CIP-0104.

Introduction

Featured App status exists to align network rewards with real economic contribution, composability, and durable utility on the Canton Network. To encourage innovation, Featured Application rewards are open to assets and applications of all kinds, with no constraints on the type of activity. All applications may generate 1.0 rewards weight per $1 burn generated. The process to gain Featured App status is lightweight and can be obtained even before go-live in order to test rewards mechanics and economics on MainNet.

On top of general innovation, Canton Network incentivizes composition of high quality apps and assets. Composed transactions can generate more than 1.0 rewards weight per $1 burn generated. To avoid any abuse of these incentives, the added composition rewards are open only to Featured Applications that clear a high barrier of entry, and this category of applications is monitored tightly by the Featured Application Accountability Committee.

App rewards are not intended to be used outside of activity that generates user value, and Featured App status is not a mechanism to amplify rewards beyond underlying on-chain activity. Failure to adhere to this guidance may result in immediate pause of Featured App status, potentially without warning, while the Accountability Committee reviews usage.

Featured Apps are encouraged to be proactive in their communication to the Featured Application Accountability Committee if there are any significant changes.

1. Featured App Qualification Categories

Featured App qualifications are divided into categories:

  • Basic Featured Apps: Anyone can obtain this status simply by clearing the App locking requirements, and keep it absent any abuse (see 1.1).
  • Asset-Moving Featured Apps
  • Featured Assets

Each featured category reflects a distinct role in the ecosystem and must satisfy the applicable standards below. Both the requirements and rewards rules differ by category. It’s expected that both the number of categories and the requirements will evolve with Tokenomics and Accountability governance over time.

Standards and the bar for the Featured categories will increase over time and will be adjusted in conjunction with the Accountability and Tokenomics Committees.

1.1 Basic Featured App

In order to become a Basic Featured App, you must:

  1. Lock 5,000,000 CC in line with the app locking rules.
  2. Fill in the app application form at the Canton Foundation.
  3. Supply a distinct FA party to generate rewards, and a distinct operational party (or validator) that generates burn for the app. These must not be used for anything else.

As long as this guidance is implemented using Application Markers, the Canton Foundation and Accountability committee will check whether the application is real/live to avoid marker abuse. Once the guidance is implemented on a traffic basis, supplying the lock, information, and partyId will automatically lead to Basic Featured App status.

1.2 Asset-Moving Featured Apps

Basic Featured Apps in good standing that move assets as part of their operation are eligible to apply for Asset-Moving Featured App status. This designation is suitable for wallets[1], trading venues (e.g DEXes, Perp exchanges, lending protocols), and bridges.

Becoming an Asset-Moving Featured App is fairly restrictive and hinges on a range of criteria including the below, though this list is neither exhaustive nor are all items a must-have if they don’t make sense for a particular app:

  • PartyId(s) for the App Operator must be fully segregated from other functions for monitoring and accountability purposes.
  • Fee handling - Apps may not, on net, pay any of their users for generating activity.
  • Users are charged a net-positive ledger fee in aggregate per user. That means any rebate or rewards programs have to be structured and coordinated across venues and assets to make sure users are not incentivized to wash trade or farm.
  • If you want an exception, campaigns where one or multiple apps collaborate to subsidize users on a net basis must be coordinated with Accountability on a case-by-case basis.
  • Integration with the broader ecosystem
  • Assets are integrated with multiple (usually 3) independent featured wallets and trading venues.
  • Wallets support multiple (usually 3) independent featured assets and trading venues.
  • Trading venues support multiple (usually 3) independent assets and wallets.
  • Trading venues have LPs/MMs known to the Canton Foundation, providing visibility into the quality of flow and liquidity.
  • Bridges are usable from multiple (usually 3) independent featured wallets and bridges at least one featured asset.
  • Correlation is an important factor. E.g. An asset that is mechanically integrated in multiple venues, but de facto only moves in one, may not clear the bar
  • Standards Compliance
  • Asset<>App integrations via standards
  • CIP-0056 or CIP-0112
  • Wallet<>App integrations via CIP-0103, WalletConnect.
  • Operational Maturity. E.g.
  • Pre-approvals supported for assets and wallet.
  • UTXO management/Auto-merging available in wallets.
  • Security
  • Co-validation by at least 2 additional, independent Validators.
  • Recovery in case of lost nodes.
  • For the avoidance of doubt, this means the asset provider can make available the private holding data to asset owners so they can rehydrate their wallet on a new node. It does not imply any ability for the asset provider to take control of assets or keys.
  • Undergo a full security audit
  • Audit report shared with Canton Foundation Tokenomics Committee
  • Open Access:
  • Sign ups must be ungated as long as network-wide scaling limits are not in the way and there are no legitimate business reasons for gating (e.g. KYC or geographic restrictions). That means by default users must be able to get invite codes automatically, or a queueing system must be in place.

In general, apps seeking Asset-Moving Featured App status must satisfy the requirements before submitting their application. Exceptions may be made where apps present a plan with a clear delivery date to add still-missing capabilities and a legitimate need for an exception.

1.3 Featured Assets

Featured Assets are high quality assets used for settlement across the ecosystem. Their criteria are roughly the same as those for Featured Apps, but with these additions:

  • Locked CC must be 25,000,000 CC in line with FA locking guidance.
  • This is per asset FA party, which in turn should be considered in light of the extra rewards available for DvPs (see 2.2.3)
  • The net-fee rule also applies to Featured Assets. Featured Assets may not incentivize users or counterparties of a magnitude that moving the asset in other apps becomes fee net-negative.
  • Security
  • Should users hold assets in a wallet that shuts down all its validators, the Assets must be recoverable by or through the Issuer from private keys only.

By default, a featured asset party is for a singular asset (InstrumentId in CIP-0056 terms). Exceptions are possible, but may be tied to lowered weight (see 4.), and such exceptions must be approved by the Featured App Accountability Committee before any markers are issued for additional assets.
In particular, in the context of protocols that bridge or wrap assets, the default model is that the protocol is a Featured app of some type and each individual emitted asset needs a separate Featured Asset status with separate 25,000,000 CC lock and partyId.

Examples of exceptions to the single-asset rule are listed below. They are for guidance only. The list is neither exhaustive, nor is there a guarantee that exceptions are granted.

  1. Equities in a particular market, representing potentially 100s of ISINs.
  2. Sovereign debt in a particular market (e.g. T-Bills), representing 100s of ISINs.
  3. Oil futures, representing 100s of ISINs
  4. Tokenized precious metals.

1.4 Other/Exceptions

There may be extenuating circumstances that merit an exception to these qualifications. Regulatory restrictions and requirements are likely to reduce the thresholds required.

Exceptions will be considered on a case-by-case basis by the Featured App Accountability & Tokenomics Committees.

2. Rewards Rules

2.1 Featured Apps

All Featured Apps - both Basic and Asset-Handling - are eligible for 1.0 rewards weight for their FA party per $1 burned by their operational party/validator.


Before CIP-0104, this is obtained by setting 1.0 marker weight per $1 burn. Post CIP-0104, this will be automated at the protocol level.

2.1.1 Timeline requirements

Markers must be submitted within two reward rounds of the burn they correspond to. If a Featured App misses this window, the opportunity to earn rewards for that activity is forfeited. The point of burn may be taken either to be the transaction that burns the CC, OR the transaction that uses the resulting traffic. For apps that top up traffic little and often, this makes no difference.

Featured apps may not pre-purchase excessive amounts of traffic and set markers for it. Specifically: Burn is only eligible for markers if the Validator’s traffic balance is less than its total traffic consumption over the preceding 24 hours. 

Submitting markers long after fees were generated, or using traffic long after it was purchased:

  • Alters the Featured App reward distribution profile
  • Distorts expectations for other participants
  • Introduces instability into reward forecasting

Markers are intended to reflect contemporaneous economic activity.

2.1.2 Tolerances

Pre-0104, there is a 15% tolerance on the marker/burn ratio. Apps that exceed 15% on a 24h basis may have their Featured status paused without warning. Apps that exceed 3% on a 30d basis may have their Featured status paused without warning.

2.1.3 No Net-Paying Users for Activity

Featured Apps may not use rewards to net pay users for activity.

Permitted:

  • Offsetting legitimate user costs
  • Reducing fees
  • Supporting real usage

Not permitted:

  • Paying users more than their costs
  • Buying artificial transaction volume
  • Creating circular activity designed primarily to farm rewards

Markers must reflect organic demand, not purchased activity.

2.1.4 Other Reasons for Pause/Review

Other reasons an app may be paused and/or reviewed:

  1. Runaway activity. Sudden unexplained spikes in activity that dilute the rewards pool significantly.
  1. >5% delta in pool impact from preceding day
  2. >100% activity delta from preceding day
  1. Overage optimization.
  1. Clear correlation between exhausting the tolerance (15%) and app rewards per marker. The marker to burn ratio should be locked in at 1:1 exactly and spikes to 15% for an app that’s otherwise running at target ratio will be reviewed.
  1. Stretching tolerances.
  1. The tolerances are strict. There is no elasticity for free traffic or any such.
  1. Sustained rewards activity without user activity.
  1. Apps that are observed to continuously burn while there is no clear interaction of users with the app will come under review.

2.1.4 Wallets as Featured Apps

Wallets are admissible as Featured Apps. However, they are eligible for app rewards only for traffic generated by their operational parties - e.g. for UTXO merges, preapproval renewals or other automations they run.

User-submitted transactions are handled thus:

  1. Under the marker regime, the wallet may set an app marker for the burn.
  2. After traffic-based app rewards come into effect, app rewards for such transactions are distributed as per section 2.3. The wallet should not be involved as an app in such transactions and thus gets no app rewards.

2.2 Featured Assets Within Asset Handling Apps

Transactions in which Featured Assets are composed within Featured Apps (Wallets, Venues, Bridges) are given a small edge:

  1. Transactions involving a single Featured Asset Party or CC movement: total 1.10 rewards weight per $1 burn. 1.01 in the case of CC movement after accounting for the 10% weight from section 4.
  2. Transactions involving two or more Featured Asset Parties or CC movement: total 1.20 rewards weight per $1 burn. 1.11 in the case of CC movement after accounting for the 10% weight from section 4.

The added weight (0.10/0.20) goes to the Featured Assets, except in the case of CC, in which case  some proportion of the CC asset rewards - see weights - go to the Featured Venue. Independent apps may enter rewards sharing agreements to change that split, as long as that is not used as an incentive for extractive behaviour and it complies with all other rules stated here (e.g. no net-paying for activity).

2.2.1 Non-Asset Featured Apps

Featured apps get the same 1.0 rewards weight as general apps for transactions where the designated validator(s)/operator party(/ies) bears the cost.

Pre-CIP-0104, the app may set the 1.0 weight themselves.

Post-0104, the protocol will assign 1.0 weight to a transaction submitted by an app and involving a featured asset.

2.2.3 Featured Assets

Featured assets get rewards weight when transacted in a Featured App:

  • 0.10 weight for a transaction moving a single Featured Asset (or CC).
  • 0.20/N for a transaction moving N Featured Assets (or CC).
  • Post-CIP-0104, the formula may be adjusted to 0.20*weight(asset) where the weight(asset) is calculated according to the CIP-0104 attribution such that it adds up to 1.0 for all assets in the transaction.

In other words, the assets split the rewards between the Venue’s weight and 1.10/1.20 weight for a single/multi-asset transaction.

Pre-CIP-0104, the assets may set the weights themselves on markers.

Post-CIP-0104, the protocol will assign weight to a transaction submitted by a Featured App and involving a Featured Asset.

2.3 User-Submitted Transactions

Transactions not submitted by an app (e.g. by one of the 500 validators that are not apps) are considered user-submitted.

For user-submitted transactions, rewards are distributed to involved featured apps according to CIP-0104 formulas. This applies only once app rewards are traffic based. Under the marker regime, wallets may set an app marker for such transactions.

2.4 Pauses/Reasons For Unfeaturing

Featured apps may be paused or relegated to Unfeatured (meaning non-Featured) status for a number of reasons, but generally with a lower bar:

  1. Not following all the rules for General Apps
  2. Activity not corresponding to genuine user activity.
  3. Overinflating activity - e.g. by running unnecessary UTXO merges, or by allowing bots/wash trading.
  4. Incentivized user activity, including by collusion with other featured apps/assets so that a cycle of wallets/assets/venues allows users to generate net-negative fees.
  5. Any other activity that is considered extractive or not in the spirit of rewarding/incentivizing genuine and valuable user activity.
  1. If in doubt, check with Accountability proactively.
  1. Unnecessary transaction sharding/batching to optimize rewards
  1. E.g. A venue mixing independent settlements of two assets to make it appear as a DvP.

2.5 Rewards distribution

2.1-2.3 cover how application rewards weight is attributed.

During the pre-CIP-0104 marker based rewards, app and asset rewards scale proportional to each other.

Post-CIP-0104, the per-round rewards are distributed such that asset rewards diminish before app rewards. Given an app reward pool of size p in a given round and w_asset total asset rewards weight and w_app total app rewards weight across assets and apps, the rewards per weight are:

App reward per 1.0 weight = min (cap, p/w_app)

The total rewards to apps (t_app) are the rewards thus mintable by general and featured apps.

Asset reward per 1.0 weight = min (cap, (p-t_app)/w_asset)

cap in the above is the app rewards cap per weight that is set to $1.50 at the time of writing. Through a CIP process parallel to this guidance change, that cap may change to $0.79 so that for a general app, maximum validator + app rewards are $0.99 per $1 burn.

2.6 Transition

As this guidance takes effect, the statuses will be applied as follows:

  1. By default, all existing featured apps will become basic featured apps.
  2. Featured apps with “DvP status” will transition to “Asset-Moving App” status.
  3. Featured assets representing singular assets will remain featured assets.
  4. Featured assets representing multiple assets will be considered on a case-by-case basis. Providers will be engaged by FAAC to discuss transition.

3. Mutual Accountability

Featured Apps and Assets must keep each other mutually accountable. Featured Apps have best visibility into the practices for their Asset counterparts, and Featured Assets have best visibility into the practices, patterns, and volumes in their App counterparts.

Should one Featured App observe behaviour that indicates non-compliance with this guidance in one of their counterparts, they must report it to Accountability. In cases of non-compliance, both sides of an arrangement are going to be under scrutiny.

Featured Apps found to be rewards sharing with their counterparts are particularly likely to be considered out of compliance if they do not report non-compliance and their counterparties are found to be materially out of compliance.

4. Exceptions

There may be extenuating circumstances that merit an exception to this guidance. Exceptions will be considered on a case-by-case basis by the Accountability or Tokenomics Committees.

4.1 Featured App Weights

Accountability and Tokenomics may on an exceptional basis grant weight to an FA party. This weight - e.g. 50% - applies to the special Featured Asset rewards rules, not the basic App rewards:

  • An Asset with weight 75% moving in a venue with weight 50% gets 0.0375 weight instead of 0.1 weight per $1 burn.

4.1.1 CC’s Asset Weight

A new governance parameter will set the weight for Canton Coin as a featured asset. The featured asset rewards for Canton Coin go to the asset handling apps moving it. Giving Canton Coin full weight might over-incentivize venues to move CC over other assets whereas not emitting rewards for CC might incentivize venues to not move CC.

The right parameter for the asset weight assigned to Canton Coin is economically equivalent for venues to an app reward share from other assets. The parameter will start at 10%, but can be updated by Tokenomics.

6. Enforcement

Failure to adhere to this guidance may result in:

  • Immediate pause of Featured App status
  • Immediate pause of Featured Asset status
  • Pause without prior warning
  • Review by the Accountability Committee
  • Potential revocation of Featured App or Featured Asset status

The objective is to preserve:

  • Burn/Mint equilibrium
  • Appropriate reward attribution
  • Composability across the ecosystem
  • Stability of reward distribution
  • Long-term tokenomic integrity

[1] Meaning the wallet’s operational party that does pre-approval renewal, UTXO merging, etc. Not user parties.
